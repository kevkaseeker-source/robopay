First survey results: do people want machines that pay?

First round, 29 Sep to 3 Oct 2026. Shared at the GIIC event, at Product Bootcamp (Superteam Germany) and in friend groups. Questions in German and English.

38valid responses (28 DE, 10 EN)
71%accept a small delivery robot at the door
63%trust a machine to release payment on delivery
86%would pay some extra for fast, automatic delivery (32 of 37)
Read this first. A small convenience sample, not a representative one: 27 of 38 are aged 18 to 24, only 6 use crypto regularly, and answers are stated intentions, not behavior. We treat this as a first demand signal and as design input. More responses are coming.

More findings

  • Willingness to pay: 32 of 37 would pay a premium; 26 of 37 at least €1 (hypothetical). 5 would pay nothing extra.
  • Who is responsible for an AI mistake? 22 of 38 say the company that built the AI, 9 say both together, 5 say themselves, 2 say nobody.
  • Which trigger do people accept? GPS arrival 10, AI judges by camera 8, both equally 9, neither 10 (37 answers). No clear winner, so offering several trust models makes sense.
  • Jobs: 8 of 38 would be less willing to use it because it could replace delivery drivers, 8 would be more willing, 22 say no change or never thought about it.
  • Crypto familiarity: 6 use it regularly, 14 tried it once, 16 heard of it, 2 never heard of it. 3 have built something on a blockchain.

What we do with this

  • Instant notification and cancellation stay hard requirements (92% and 84%).
  • Only 47% see blockchain payment as equally safe, so end users should buy the outcome, not the chain.
  • 43% are bothered by a permanent public delivery location. We should not put exact addresses on-chain.
  • 58% would be put off by GPS spoofing and opinions on triggers are split. This supports offering several ways to prove a delivery.
  • Agent shopping needs a hard spending cap, enforced in code, and opt-in.

Method and limits

Google Forms, about 25 five-point and multiple-choice questions plus open text. One duplicate submission removed. DE and EN answers are pooled without weighting, no significance tests. No names or e-mail addresses are published here. Raw data stays with the team.
